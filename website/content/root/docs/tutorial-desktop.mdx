In this lesson we do exactly one thing: **build a working weekend book-club sign-up page inside an empty folder.**

No commands to memorise, no programming knowledge required. You click a few times, then describe what you want the way you would to a colleague.

## <span class="uc-step-no">01</span> Open the desktop window and add your empty folder

Open the desktop app, click **Add project** in the sidebar, and pick the empty folder you prepared. Keeping it near your Desktop or Documents is easiest.

## <span class="uc-step-no">02</span> Describe it the way you would to a colleague

One complete sentence is enough: **who it is for, what it must do, and how you will check it.** Those three things cut most of the rework later.

**What you'll see:** Instead of guessing and writing immediately, it asks a few decisive questions first, using choice cards you can click.

## <span class="uc-step-no">03</span> Answer the cards, then let it check its own work

When a choice card appears, click whichever option matches your intent; if you are unsure, take the recommended one. Then it moves forward in small steps, and you can interrupt at any moment.

**What you'll see:** After building a first version it opens a real browser, clicks the buttons, checks both desktop and phone layouts, and reports back. Accept it once you see that check pass.

## <span class="uc-step-no">04</span> Not happy? Keep talking instead of starting over

This is the difference between describing a **goal** and writing code: you describe the outcome, and it handles implementation plus self-checking.

<CommandBlock
  label="Ask for a style change like this"

  mono={false}
  copyLabel="Copy"
  copiedLabel="Copied"
/>

**What you'll see:** Only the colours and layout change; the sign-up logic you already approved stays intact.
