The first two lessons covered **how to start**. This one is about **how to avoid wasted work**.

Three moves: agree on the plan, build it, then make it verify itself.

## <span class="uc-step-no">01</span> Switch to the plan-only role first

Press `Shift + Tab` to switch. The most expensive mistake is starting to edit before the approach is settled — this step prevents exactly that.

**What you'll see:** The role at the bottom changes from UltraBuild to UltraPlan. In that role it only reads and writes plans; your files stay untouched.

## <span class="uc-step-no">02</span> Have it spell out risks and trade-offs

The output here is not code; it is **the standard you both agreed on**. Everything later is judged against it.

<CommandBlock
  label="Send this sentence"
  command="Do not write code yet. Tell me how many steps this sign-up page needs, how each step will be verified, and where it is easy to get wrong. List the open questions for me."
  mono={false}
  copyLabel="Copy"

**What you'll see:** A step-by-step plan with acceptance checks, plus the questions that genuinely need your decision.

## <span class="uc-step-no">03</span> Approve it, then switch back and build

Keep refining while you are not satisfied. Once you are, switching back makes it follow the plan instead of improvising.

**What you'll see:** The role returns to UltraBuild and it implements the agreed plan step by step.

## <span class="uc-step-no">04</span> Let it click through the real page

Self-checking is where quality quietly slips, so what you want here is **someone else verifying**. That is the default way UltraCode works.

<CommandBlock
  label="Ask for verification and an independent review"
  command="When it is built, open a real browser and click through both the desktop and phone layouts: buttons work, nothing overflows, no console errors. Then have an independent reviewer check it and fix anything it finds."
  mono={false}
  copyLabel="Copy"

**What you'll see:** It actually operates the page and takes screenshots, fixing what it finds; then a separate reviewer role returns a pass or fail verdict.
