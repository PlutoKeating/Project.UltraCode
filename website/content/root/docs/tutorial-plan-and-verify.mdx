The first two lessons covered **how to start**. This one is about **how to avoid wasted work**.

Three moves: agree on the plan, build it, then make it verify itself.

<TutorialStep
  step={1}
  title="Switch to the plan-only role first"
  expectLabel="What you'll see"
  expectText="The role at the bottom changes from UltraBuild to UltraPlan. In that role it only reads and writes plans; your files stay untouched."
>
  Press `Shift + Tab` to switch. The most expensive mistake is starting to edit before the approach is settled — this step prevents exactly that.

<TutorialStep
  step={2}
  title="Have it spell out risks and trade-offs"
  promptLabel="Send this sentence"
  promptText="Do not write code yet. Tell me how many steps this sign-up page needs, how each step will be verified, and where it is easy to get wrong. List the open questions for me."
  copyLabel="Copy"
  copiedLabel="Copied"
  expectLabel="What you'll see"
  expectText="A step-by-step plan with acceptance checks, plus the questions that genuinely need your decision."
>
  The output here is not code; it is **the standard you both agreed on**. Everything later is judged against it.
</TutorialStep>

<TutorialStep
  step={3}
  title="Approve it, then switch back and build"
  expectLabel="What you'll see"
  expectText="The role returns to UltraBuild and it implements the agreed plan step by step."
>
  Keep refining while you are not satisfied. Once you are, switching back makes it follow the plan instead of improvising.
</TutorialStep>

<TutorialStep
  step={4}
  title="Let it click through the real page"
  promptLabel="Ask for verification and an independent review"
  promptText="When it is built, open a real browser and click through both the desktop and phone layouts: buttons work, nothing overflows, no console errors. Then have an independent reviewer check it and fix anything it finds."
  copyLabel="Copy"
  copiedLabel="Copied"
  expectLabel="What you'll see"
  expectText="It actually operates the page and takes screenshots, fixing what it finds; then a separate reviewer role returns a pass or fail verdict."
>
  Self-checking is where quality quietly slips, so what you want here is **someone else verifying**. That is the default way UltraCode works.
</TutorialStep>
